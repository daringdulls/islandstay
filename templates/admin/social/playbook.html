Social media playbook

How Island Stay shows up on social: what we post, when, and how it should look and sound. The calendar's “Draft plan” button follows this plan.

1. Goal

Get travellers to open an island guide and message a local host. Every post points to one island, one host or one experience, and ends with a way to reach it.

  • Travellers: independent travellers, divers and surfers, couples and families looking for local-island trips at guest house prices.
  • Hosts: guest house owners, dive centres, boat operators and cafés who could list on Island Stay.

An attractive grid

  • The grid alternates photo tiles and solid-colour tiles. With three columns that makes a checkerboard, so no two photos or two colour blocks touch.
  • Colour tiles rotate light (ivory, Mist), dark (Channel, Lagoon) and warm (Sandbank) so each row has contrast.
  • Instagram crops the grid to 3:4. Keep the headline away from the very top and bottom of Reel covers; the feed preview shows the crop.
  • Use the feed preview before scheduling: drag tiles to swap their dates, and fix any tile outlined in red (same template next to itself).
  • Pin three posts to the top of the profile: Our story, the latest top five carousel and the newest Reel.
  • Story highlights: Start here, Islands, Stays, Getting there, Experiences, Tips, Dhivehi, Hosts. Add each story to its highlight after 24 hours. Covers: download the highlight covers (use one set, dark or light, for all of them).

Reels

  • 15–30 seconds, vertical, a hook on screen in the first 2 seconds, captions on screen (most people watch muted).
  • Real footage from hosts and guests: the boat ride, the reef, breakfast on the jetty. Ask before filming people.
  • Use the Reel design as the cover and the end card. Share every Reel to Stories and to TikTok.

Design rules

  • Text never sits straight on a photo: it goes on a solid Channel, Sandbank, Mist, Lagoon or Shell Ivory panel.
  • One accent word per headline, in Lagoon on light grounds and Shallows on dark grounds. Never Shallows text on a light ground.
  • Cowrie Gold appears in two places only: the logo ring, and booking buttons (“Send a request”, “Book”) with Ink text. Prices use Gold Text #9A5F05 on ivory. Nothing else is gold.
  • Coral is for hearts and illustration only. Every design needs one warm element: a Sandbank panel, a gold booking pill or a coral heart.
  • The plain cowrie (no ring) only marks verified hosts.
  • One pattern per design, never behind body text: cowrie scatter (Lagoon), atoll chart (ivory), shoreline waves (Channel) or the feyli stripe.
  • Pill buttons and chips; rounded photo corners. No emoji on the design itself.
  • Real islands and real hosts in warm natural light. No stock overwater villas.

Logo

  • Always use the logo files. Never retype, recolour, rotate, stretch, outline or add shadows.
  • Light grounds (ivory, Sandbank, Mist): the colour logo for light. Channel grounds: the colour logo for dark. Lagoon grounds: the on-lagoon logo.
  • The ring is always gold. Keep clear space of half the symbol's width; never smaller than 120 px wide.
  • Use the symbol on its own for the profile picture and for the “Our story” post.

Our story (use as written)

  • The atoll: the ring is a Maldivian atoll. “Atoll” comes from the Dhivehi word atholhu, the one Dhivehi word the world borrowed.
  • The kandu: the wide gap is the channel boats sail through: the way in for guests, the way out for island life.
  • The cowrie: for centuries the Maldives supplied the cowrie shells used as money across Asia and Africa. The cowrie points out through the kandu: the islands open to the world again, with money going straight to local hosts.
  • Short version: “An atoll, a channel and a cowrie: the Maldives, open to you, local all the way.”
  • Taglines: “Local to you.” · “Stay where the island lives.” · “Message a local. Live the island.”

6. Voice and captions

  • Warm, direct and practical, like a host on the jetty. “You” for the reader, “we” for Island Stay. Sentence case.
  • Lead with the island or the person. Show the specific thing: “a 3 km sandbank tail”, not “a hidden gem”.
  • Don't use: paradise, hidden gem, luxury escape, unforgettable.
  • Explain a Maldivian word the first time: dhoni (boat), kandu (channel), finolhu (sandbank).
  • At most one emoji per caption. No prices unless the host has agreed, written as “from $85 / night”.
  • Lead with the island and the person: “Stay with Aishath on Dhangethi”.

Caption formula

  1. Hook: one line about the place or person.
  2. Detail: one or two useful facts (getting there, what it's known for).
  3. Next step: “Read the island guide” or “Send a request, no booking fees”, then “Link in bio”.
  4. Hashtags: 5 to 8. Always #IslandStay #Maldives #LocalIslands, plus the island, the atoll and the activity.

7. Workflow

  1. Draft: press “Draft plan” on the calendar once a month. It drafts six posts a week from the directory.
  2. Review: open each design, check the facts, swap the photo if a better one exists, edit the caption.
  3. Ready: mark it ready. Download the PNG and copy the caption.
  4. Scheduled: schedule it in Meta Business Suite (Instagram + Facebook) or the platform's own scheduler, then mark it scheduled.
  5. Posted: after it goes out, mark it posted and paste the post link.

Before posting

  • Hosts: ask the owner before featuring them, and use their own photos when possible.
  • Wikimedia photos: credit the photographer in the caption when the licence is CC BY or CC BY-SA (shown on the design page).
  • Check local rules and transfer times against the island guide.
