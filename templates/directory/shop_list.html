Made in the Maldives

Six things worth taking home

{% icon "sparkles" %}

Lacquer work

Liyelaa jehun

Wooden boxes and vases turned and coated in red, yellow and black lacquer, a craft from Baa Atoll.

{% icon "leaf" %}

Woven mats

Thundu kunaa

Fine reed mats woven by women in Gaafu Dhaalu, with natural dyes and geometric patterns.

{% icon "sailboat" %}

Dhoni models

Hand carved

Small wooden copies of the traditional Maldivian boat, made by island boat builders.

{% icon "fish" %}

Rihaakuru and dried tuna

Food

Thick tuna paste and smoked dried fish (Maldive fish), the base of many island dishes.

{% icon "cherry" %}

Local sweets

Bondi and huni hakuru

Coconut and sugar sweets, often wrapped in leaves, sold at island shops and cafés.

{% icon "shirt" %}

Island-made clothing

Sarongs and prints

Light sarongs and shirts, useful for covering up when you walk through the village.

{# ---------- tips + CTA ---------- #}
Good to know

Shopping on a local island

  • {% icon "shield-check" %}Don't buy coral, shells or turtle shell. Taking them out of the country is against the law.
  • {% icon "clock" %}Fridays: many shops close for midday prayers, and some close briefly at other prayer times.
  • {% icon "banknote" %}Bring some cash. Small shops may only take rufiyaa or US dollars.
  • {% icon "heart-handshake" %}Buy local: ask whether a souvenir was made in the Maldives. Many are imported.
