Maldivian food to try

Six things to order on a local island

{% icon "sun" %}

Mas huni

Breakfast

Tuna, grated coconut, onion and chilli, eaten with warm roshi flatbread and sweet black tea.

{% icon "soup" %}

Garudhiya

Lunch or dinner

A clear tuna broth served with rice, lime, chilli and onion. The everyday family meal.

{% icon "coffee" %}

Hedhikaa

Tea time

Short eats: a tray of fried and baked snacks such as gulha, bajiya and fish rolls, with tea.

{% icon "fish" %}

Fihunu mas

Dinner

Fish rubbed with chilli paste and grilled, often reef fish caught that day.

{% icon "utensils" %}

Mas riha

Lunch or dinner

Tuna curry, rich with coconut and curry leaves, eaten with rice or roshi.

{% icon "citrus" %}

Fresh juice

Any time

Watermelon, papaya, lime and young coconut (kurumba): the drinks of an alcohol-free island.

Good to know

Eating on a local island

  • {% icon "shield-check" %}No alcohol is served on local islands. Try a resort day trip or a safari boat if you'd like a drink.
  • {% icon "clock" %}Fridays: many cafés close for midday prayers and reopen in the afternoon.
  • {% icon "calendar" %}During Ramadan cafés open after sunset; guest houses still serve guests during the day.
  • {% icon "banknote" %}Bring some cash. Small cafés may not take cards, and some add a 10% service charge.
  • {% icon "heart-handshake" %}Ask your host: many guest houses cook dinner on request, including beach barbecues.
